Researchers Chen and Abara studied whether a breathing app could help people with mild insomnia fall asleep faster. They recruited 412 adults with mild insomnia who were having difficulty falling asleep. The participants were divided into two groups: half used a breathing app designed to promote relaxation and induce sleep, while the other half continued with their usual sleep routines.

For eight weeks, the app group followed the app’s guided breathing exercises before bed. At the end of the eight weeks, the researchers found that the group using the breathing app fell asleep an average of nine minutes faster than the control group. This suggests a small but measurable benefit for sleep onset.

But there are some key limitations. The study didn’t track participants for longer than eight weeks, so it’s unknown whether the benefit would last. Also, 37 participants dropped out of the study, which could have introduced some bias into the results.

Still, the findings offer a preliminary indication that breathing exercises delivered through a mobile app might be a simple and accessible tool for mild insomnia. Further research with longer-term follow-up is needed to confirm these results and understand how effective the approach is in the long term.
