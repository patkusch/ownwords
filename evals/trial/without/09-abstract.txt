Chen and Abara (2024) tested whether a breathing app helps people with mild insomnia fall asleep faster.

They recruited 412 adults with mild insomnia. Half used the app, doing its guided breathing exercises before bed. The other half kept to their usual routine. The trial lasted eight weeks.

At the end, the app group fell asleep nine minutes faster on average than the comparison group. That is a small effect, but a measurable one.

Two things limit how far the result can be trusted. The study stopped at eight weeks, so nobody knows whether the benefit lasts. And 37 participants dropped out, which may have skewed the result.

The fair reading is that a breathing app may help a little with mild insomnia. A longer study is needed before anyone can say more than that.
